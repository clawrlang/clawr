# ADR-021: Binary Operators and Precedence

[ADRs](./README.md)

## Status

💡 **Proposed**

> An idea to be considered for adoption

## Context

Binary operators are a staple of programming. This is generally how calculations are done in computers.

Operator notation requires an order-of-precedence to be unambiguous. When the intent is to perform operations in a different order than the default (or to emphasize the order for clarity), the strategy is typically to group operands in parentheses.

## Decision

Clawr operators apply in the following order (low to high precedence):

1. Logical OR (`||`)
2. Logical AND (`&&`)
3. Comparison (`==`, `!=`, `<`, `>`…)
4. Field OR ( `|`)
5. Field AND ( `&`)
6. Shift operator (`<<`, `>>`)
7. Additive (`+`, `-`)
8. Multiplicative (`*`, `/`)
9. Exponential (`^`)
10. Postfix member access (`.`, `->`) and function/method call (`()`)
11. Prefix ( `!`)
12. Parentheses, literals and identifiers

The frontend calculates the result-set (domain + current value) of each operation individually based on the behaviour of the operator.

## Rationale

- Field operators precede comparison operators. Where C would treat `x & 5 == 5` with confusion, Clawr should chose the sensical interpretation.
- Shift operators precede logical field operators according to established prior art. There is no reason to contradict it.

## Consequences

### Negative

- Programmers used to C might find the ordering inconsistent and confusing.

### Positive

- The ordering is expected to appeal to logical thinkers.
